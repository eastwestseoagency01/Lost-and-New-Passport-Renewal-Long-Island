Passport basics that save time

A little knowledge goes a long way. In general, adults can renew a passport without starting over if the passport was issued at age 16 or older, is undamaged, is in their possession, and was issued within the last fifteen years. Renewals are usually simpler and require fewer documents. If your passport was lost, stolen, or damaged, or if you do not meet the renewal rules, you typically need to apply as a new applicant with proof of citizenship and identification.

Photos are another common stumbling block. A passport photo must meet strict size, background, and lighting rules, and many applications are delayed because a photo was rejected. Signatures, payment methods, and name changes can also cause problems. We review each of these details with you, so your application is complete and consistent. Remember that many countries require at least six months of validity beyond your travel dates, so it is wise to renew earlier than you think you need to.

Do you issue passports?

No. Passports are issued only by the U.S. Department of State. We are a private service that helps you prepare, review, and submit your application and guides you through your options.

Can you guarantee same day passport renewal?

No private company can guarantee same day issuance. Same day passports are possible only in limited urgent cases at government passport agencies. We can offer same day consultations and fast application preparation, and we explain honestly what may be available.

What should I do if my passport is lost or stolen?

Report it right away and apply for a replacement. A lost or stolen passport generally cannot be renewed by mail, so you will need a new application with proof of citizenship and identification. We guide you through each step.

Can I renew my passport if it is damaged?

Usually not by mail. A damaged passport typically requires a new application. We review its condition and explain which documents you will need.

How long does passport renewal take?

Processing times are set by the U.S. Department of State and change often. Expedited handling is faster than routine service. We recommend starting as early as possible and we will explain current options when you call.
